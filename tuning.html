FAQ

Tuning Questions

How often should I have my piano tuned?
Twice a year is the standard recommendation for most home pianos — once in the fall when heat comes on and once in the spring when humidity changes. Pianos in heavily used settings like churches, schools, and performance venues typically benefit from tuning every 3–4 months. If your piano hasn't been tuned in several years, it will likely need a pitch raise before a full tuning can hold.
What's included in the $175 tuning?
A complete tuning to A440 concert pitch. If the piano has drifted significantly flat, a pitch raise is included at no extra charge — we bring the strings back up to proper tension first, then tune. The visit typically takes 60–90 minutes depending on the piano's condition.
What is voicing and do I need it?
Voicing adjusts the tone quality of individual notes by modifying the density and shape of the hammer felts. If your piano sounds too bright and harsh, or dull and muted, voicing can bring it back into balance. It's separate from tuning — a piano can be perfectly in tune and still benefit from voicing. Christian can assess whether voicing would help your instrument at the tuning visit.
How does the bulk rate work?
The bulk rate of $125 per tuning (and $75/hr for voicing) applies when you schedule 4 or more pianos. They don't all have to be tuned in a single day — we can spread visits across your schedule while still applying the bulk rate. Just let us know how many pianos you have when you reach out and we'll quote accordingly.
Do you come to us or do we bring the piano somewhere?
We come to you. All tuning is done in-home or on-site at your location throughout North Georgia. There's no need to move your piano.
Can you tune player pianos too?
Yes. The acoustic side of a player piano is tuned the same way as any other piano. If you also need player system repair — the pneumatics, bellows, or roll mechanism — that's handled separately by our player piano technician. We can coordinate both in a single visit. See our player piano repair page for details.
